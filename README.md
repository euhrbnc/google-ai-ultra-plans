# Google AI Ultra 20x: What the 20x Usage Limit and 30TB Plan Actually Get You, and How to Pay for It Without a US Card

Google did something at I/O that almost nobody outside the subscription screens noticed: it turned "AI Ultra" into two different plans that share the same name. So if you searched for Google AI Ultra 20x, you were probably trying to work out which one is which — and whether the $100 difference buys you anything real.

Short version: 20x is the $199.99/month tier. It gives you 20 times the usage allowance of the $19.99 Pro plan in the Gemini app and Google Antigravity, plus 30TB of Google One storage, YouTube Premium, and a few features the cheaper tiers don't get. The other plan, at $99.99/month, is capped at 5x with 20TB. Both are called AI Ultra.

## Why "Google AI Ultra 20x" is now a specification, not a nickname

Before the I/O announcement, there was one Ultra plan at $249.99/month. Google cut that to $199.99 and kept every capability, then slid a $100 tier underneath it. The multipliers became the only clean way to tell them apart — which is why Google's own support page now describes the membership as a choice between "Google AI Ultra 5x (20 TB of storage)" and "Google AI Ultra 20x (30 TB of storage)."

That naming problem was real enough that Google had to fix the upgrade screen. Vikas Kansal, who leads Gemini AI subscriptions, confirmed the flow now shows AI usage next to storage when you compare plans, instead of hiding the difference behind a doubled price tag and a bit more disk space. The plans still share a name.

## What "20x" is actually measuring

Google standardized the multipliers across tiers: 5x and 20x are both expressed relative to the $20 Pro plan, and the same ratio applies during promotional periods when Google has spare capacity. That's the definition straight from Google's Antigravity documentation.

What it does not mean is twenty times the number of messages. Google moved from daily prompt caps to a compute-used model. Your allowance now depends on how complex the prompt is, which features you're touching, and how long the conversation runs. A one-line question costs a sliver; a video render or an agentic coding run can eat a meaningful chunk.

The mechanics that matter in practice:

- Limits refresh every five hours until you hit a weekly cap.
- If you burn through your allowance on the biggest models, Google doesn't lock you out — it drops you to smaller, faster models so you keep working.
- Pro and Ultra members can buy pay-as-you-go AI credits for Google Flow and Google Antigravity, with the Gemini app on the roadmap.

So 20x is a bigger bucket measured in compute, not a promise of more prompts. If your work is mostly text back-and-forth, you will struggle to fill either bucket. If it's video generation, Deep Research or long coding sessions, the multiplier is the thing you're actually paying for.

## Everything riding along with the 20x plan

The $199.99 tier isn't only about the limit. Here's what's in it:

- 30TB of Google One storage across Drive, Gmail and Photos
- YouTube Premium, the individual plan
- Project Genie, the experimental world-building tool, now rolling out globally to $200-tier subscribers aged 18 and up, with a Street View capability that anchors generated worlds to real locations
- Gemini Spark, the always-on agent that moves across Google services and acts on your behalf — US subscribers on both Ultra plans get it in beta
- Deep Think, Veo video, Google Flow and Flow Music, Jules, Google Antigravity, and Google AI Studio
- Gemini inside Gmail, Docs and Vids, plus AI Inbox in Gmail and the Daily Brief digest
- Health Premium and Home Premium, folded in at no extra cost
- Family group sharing: members on your plan get selected AI benefits without paying separately

One thing that isn't a reason to pay more: model access. Gemini Omni and Gemini 3.5 Flash rolled out to every paid tier, Plus and Pro included. The differentiator between the plans is limits, storage, and the Ultra-only extras — not which model you can talk to.

## Who should actually pay for 20x

The honest answer is: fewer people than the name suggests.

Take the 20x tier if you're running Antigravity or Flow hard, if your day includes Veo renders or long agentic coding runs, and if the extra 10TB over the 5x plan is storage you'd otherwise buy separately. Project Genie is exclusive to this tier, so if you want to play with it, that's your only route.

Take the 5x tier if you're a developer or technical lead who hits limits regularly but not constantly. ZDNET's read on it is the same as mine: start cheaper and move up if you keep bouncing off the ceiling, since switching plans is supported. You'd be giving up 10TB of storage and Project Genie, and you'd cut your bill in half.

Stay on Pro at $19.99 if what you actually wanted was better models and a bigger allowance than the free tier. Both new models landed on Pro too, and Pro subscribers in some countries are getting YouTube Premium Lite bundled at no extra cost.

One criticism worth keeping in mind: the compute-based model swaps a clear, countable limit for a fuzzy one. Your prompt's "complexity" is the meter, and that's a number Google controls. Nothing stops the same $199.99 from buying less compute over time without any announcement. That's not a reason to avoid the plan; it's a reason not to sign an annual commitment you can't get out of.

The storage math is simpler. The 20x plan's headline advantage over the 5x plan is 10 extra terabytes. If you don't have 10TB of files, you're paying $100 a month for compute and Project Genie.

## The restrictions that show up after you've paid

Google's own support documentation lists situations where you cannot upgrade to AI Ultra at all:

- You're an existing Google One member who subscribed through a third-party partner.
- You subscribed through Pixel Pass.
- You're on Google One through the Apple Store — you have to cancel first, then sign up fresh.

You also need a personal Google Account, not a work one. Several Ultra features are region-limited, and there are age floors: 18 and over for Deep Research, Chrome auto browse, Project Genie and the Gemini 3 Pro model in AI Mode; 13 or over for the Gemini app, Gemini Notebook, Earth features and the YouTube Premium individual plan.

That first bullet matters more than it looks if you're thinking about a reseller. Read it as a warning, not a dealbreaker: buying a Google AI subscription through a third party can leave your account unable to move itself up to Ultra in Google's own upgrade flow later. Confirm that before you hand over money, not after.

## Quick answers

**Does 20x mean 20 times more prompts?**
No. It's a compute allowance measured against the $20 Pro plan, so heavy tasks consume it far faster than text chats. Limits reset every five hours until you reach a weekly cap.

**Can I switch from the 5x plan to 20x later?**
Yes, Google supports changing membership plans, and the upgrade screen now shows AI usage alongside storage. The main obstacle is the third-party subscription rule above.

**Do family members get the Ultra benefits?**
Selected AI benefits and features, at no extra cost, for members of the family group on an Ultra membership.

**Is Project Genie included in both Ultra plans?**
No. It's rolling out globally to the $200 tier, for subscribers 18 and over.

**Is the 30TB worth the extra $100 over the 5x plan?**
Only if you'd otherwise buy that storage. Otherwise you're paying for compute headroom and one experimental feature.

Google AI Ultra 20x is the $199.99 tier: 20x the compute allowance of Pro, 30TB of storage, YouTube Premium, Project Genie, and the agent features that don't reach the lower plans. Google made it $50 cheaper than it used to be, which is the rare subscription change that goes in your direction.
